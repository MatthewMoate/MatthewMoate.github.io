The Impact

Creating space can change what happens next

Take a Mate began as Matthew's way of building accountability and routine through fishing. By sharing what was happening behind the scenes, he created space for other people to recognise something of themselves.

The messages started arriving

People felt less alone

People responded to the honesty in Matthew's videos and the simple act of showing up. Their messages spoke about reconnecting with fishing, getting through difficult periods and reaching out to someone.

The names behind these messages remain private. The words show why the work matters.

“I realised I wasn't the only one.”
“I picked my fishing rods back up.”
“Your videos helped me through one of the hardest periods of my life.”
“I finally reached out to someone.”

What audiences can take away

Awareness with somewhere to go

  1. 01

    Recognise what may sit beneath performance

    A clearer understanding of high-functioning struggle and why it can remain hidden.

  2. 02

    Feel more confident noticing

    Greater confidence to recognise when someone may be doing it tough.

  3. 03

    Create a useful conversation

    Practical ways to make space, listen and keep showing up.

  4. 04

    Remember a simple philosophy

    Notice. Show up. Stay. gives people language they can carry beyond the room.

Extend the conversation

The best conversations don't end when the keynote does

Workshops, leadership sessions and shared experiences give people time to turn awareness into action. The aim is not to provide a perfect script. It is to help people notice the change, create the space and stay connected.
